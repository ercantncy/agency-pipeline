Critical issues

Six checks

1
Google Ads clicks vs GA4 sessions
17% of clicks arrive as GA4 sessions

1,622 Google Ads clicks versus 269 GA4 google / cpc sessions (17%). Healthy accounts typically record 75 to 95%. Why it matters: Paid-search sessions, conversions and cost per conversion in GA4 are understated, and the missing visits are probably being counted as organic or direct.

✕Critical
2
Meta link clicks vs GA4 sessions
70% of link clicks arrive as GA4 sessions

2,784 Meta link clicks versus 1,947 GA4 paid-social sessions (70%), within the normal range.

✓Healthy
3
UTM and campaign tagging
3 Meta source labels; 0% of paid sessions untagged

Meta traffic is split over 3 source labels (facebook, fb, ig). Why it matters: Split or missing tags break campaign-level reporting: the same channel appears as several rows, and untagged paid visits cannot be tied to the spend that caused them.

▲Needs attention
4
Conversion agreement
Google Ads: 18% / Meta: 30%

Google Ads reported 109 conversions, GA4 recorded 20 key events (18%); Meta reported 105 conversions, GA4 recorded 32 key events (30%). Agreement within roughly 60 to 160% is normal. Why it matters: When the platform and GA4 disagree, nobody can say what a lead or sale really costs, and bidding algorithms optimise on the wrong signal.

✕Critical
5
Volume drops and outages
No outages found

Daily GA4 sessions stayed within a normal range for the whole period.

✓Healthy
6
Suspicious direct and organic traffic
Direct 32% of sessions; organic 1.23x GSC clicks

GA4 organic sessions are 1.23x the clicks Search Console reports while paid clicks are going missing; direct is 32% of all sessions. Why it matters: Visits that lose their source fall into direct or organic. That inflates those channels and makes paid media look weaker than it is.

✕Critical

Top fixes, in priority order

  1. Google Ads clicks vs GA4 sessions. Turn on auto-tagging in Google Ads (Account settings), confirm the Google Ads and GA4 links are active, and make sure no redirect or consent script strips the gclid parameter before GA4 loads.
  2. Conversion agreement. Check that the same conversion event is imported into Google Ads as in GA4, remove duplicate or test events, and compare attribution windows before comparing totals.
  3. Suspicious direct and organic traffic. Fix the paid-traffic tagging first (see the checks above), then re-check direct and organic. Also look for missing UTM tags on email and social links.
Next step: Reporting Pipeline Setup Connect these sources to one warehouse with tested numbers, automatic monitoring that catches issues like these within a day, and white-label client reports.
